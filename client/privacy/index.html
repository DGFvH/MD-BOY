Privacy

Hashlite stores only what it needs to keep your documents safe. No tracking, no analytics, no ads.

What is stored

  • Your email address, to identify your account when you sign in.
  • A hash of your password. The password itself is never stored.
  • Your documents and folders, including titles, content and when they were changed.
  • Images you paste, drop or upload into a document.
  • Earlier versions of your documents (version history), so you can restore them. Old automatic versions are thinned out over time.
  • Sessions: a hashed session token and its expiry date, so you stay signed in.

Your documents are private to your account. A document is only visible to others if you create a read-only share link for it, and you can turn that link off again.

Cookies

Hashlite sets one cookie: a session cookie that keeps you signed in. It is needed for the service to work. There are no tracking cookies, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party scripts, so no cookie banner is needed.

Storage in your browser

The editor uses your browser's local storage for two things: an unsaved draft of the document you are editing, so nothing is lost if your connection drops, and your preferences, such as the theme and the layout. This data stays on your device and is removed when you clear your browser's site data.

Export and delete your data

Open the Account dialog in the editor to:

  • Download all documents as a .zip of Markdown files, with your folders kept.
  • Delete your account. This permanently removes your account, documents, folders, images, versions and sessions.

You can also export a single document as Markdown, HTML or PDF from the document's menu.

Self-hosted instances

Hashlite is open source and anyone can run their own copy. If you use an instance run by someone else, that person or organisation is the data controller for it and decides where it is hosted and how it is backed up. If you self-host Hashlite, you are the data controller for your own instance.
